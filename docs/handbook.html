What this is

One app, one repo, one install.

kumar_service holds three things that used to be separate: the pump traceability and warranty engine, the dealer's own screens, and a helpdesk. Frappe Helpdesk is vendored inside the app rather than installed beside it, so there is a single product to deploy and a single place to change.

The record

A Service Request is the record of a job. It knows the serial number, the pump model, the warranty position, the heat and winding batches, the SLA clocks and which dealer it belongs to.

The queue

An HD Ticket mirrors each request into the desk so agents work a queue. It carries the pump facts and a link home. The request stays the record; the ticket is how it is worked.

Two things in, one queue out

A dealer creates a Request (typed: complaint, installation, paid service, spare part, enquiry) or a Warranty Claim. Nothing else. Every request mirrors into a ticket automatically; nobody creates a ticket by hand. A claim is separate only because it is money and needs a different person's approval.

Signing in

One login per person. Where you land depends on who you are.

You areYou land onYou can see
A dealer or distributor/kumar-desk/dealer Your own outlet and every sub-dealer beneath it. Nothing from another outlet.
KUMAR service staff/kumar-desk/manage Every dealer's tickets, the visit board, the claims desk, any serial.
Admin / manager/app and the desk Everything, plus the ERPNext side and the reports.

The sign-in page is the product's own: what it is on the left, the form on the right, and a line each for dealers, staff, and anyone who only wants to check a warranty (/warranty_check, no login). Under the skin it is Frappe's form, so forgot-password, the email-link login and any single sign-on you add later keep working untouched.

Old links still work. /dealer_portal and /helpdesk both redirect into the desk.

Register a Sale

Do this on the day you sell. The warranty starts from the registration.

1Scan or type the serial Press Scan to use the phone camera on the nameplate barcode. A USB scanner works too — it types into the box and presses Enter.
2Your invoice number and sale date Your bill to the customer, not KUMAR's bill to you.
3Who bought it Name and mobile. This is who gets rung when the warranty is running out.
4Where it is installed Village, district, application. This is the address a technician is sent to — a request has no address of its own.
The certificate

Registering produces the warranty certificate the customer keeps. Print it here, or later from any row in What I Sold.

Dealer

What I Sold

Every pump you have sold, searched in the browser.

Six filters combine — search, warranty state, product family, model, district, and a sold-between range — and each list is built from what you have actually sold, so you are never offered a filter that returns nothing.

The scan box

When you are standing next to a pump you have the barcode, not the customer's name. Scanning clears the filters first, so a dropdown left set half an hour ago cannot hide the pump you just scanned, and it tells you which customer it landed on.

Two actions per row

  • Print — the warranty certificate for that customer.
  • Complaint — opens a request with the pump already filled in.
Dealer

Raise a Request

A list of everything you have raised, with the form behind New Request.

Each row shows what was raised, which pump, when, the status, and whether KUMAR has replied — because that last one is what you are actually checking for. Filter by status, search by number, serial or model.

The form

The first question is what you need, not what is broken:

TypeUse it for
ComplaintA pump that is not working. Asks you which fault.
InstallationA new set to be fitted.
Paid ServiceA visit outside warranty. Everyone knows it is chargeable from the start.
Spare PartA part you need sent.
EnquiryAnything else.

Pick the pump — type a serial, model or customer, or press Scan — and the warranty position appears before you write anything, so you can tell the customer on the phone whether the visit is free.

Add photos or a video

This is most of what you have to offer. A picture of a burnt winding or a leaking seal decides whether a technician is sent. Up to 8 MB each, and they land on the same thread KUMAR reads.

Submitting starts the response clock. A reply from you on a request already marked resolved reopens it — you never write into a thread nobody reads.

Dealer

Warranty Claims

Your claims and how each went, with the form behind New Claim.

Name the claim type, the root cause, and what the technician found. Attach photographs of the failed part — a claim is money, and a claim with evidence is settled faster than one without.

A claim moves through Pending Review → Under Investigation → Approved → Settled. You see each step on the claim, KUMAR writes to you at each one, and you can ask a question on it at any point. Settled and Rejected are final by design.

Dealer

My Tickets

Every request and claim, and the conversation on each.

Whatever KUMAR writes here you see, and whatever you write KUMAR sees — including the photographs. When a visit is booked, the date, the technician, and whether there is anything to pay arrive on this thread. A warranty claim has a ticket of its own, typed Warranty Claim, and every decision on it lands there.

Raise for a Pump

/kumar-desk/raise — the one place to raise anything for whoever rang or wrote in.

A dealer raises from the portal. Everyone else — a pump owner on the phone, an email from a site, a distributor's WhatsApp — reaches KUMAR, and KUMAR raises it for them here. Find the pump by serial, customer name, phone, dealer, district or invoice, or scan the nameplate. The pump card shows the warranty verdict, the customer and what is already open on it, so nothing is raised twice — a chip opens the existing ticket. Say how it reached you — phone, email, WhatsApp, walk-in, field visit — and that travels onto the request and the ticket.

RaiseWhat happensWhere it lands
RequestType, what is wrong, priority, the caller's words, photos or video. Submitted, so the SLA clock starts.Its ticket; the dealer is told.
Warranty claimClaim type, amount, root cause if known, the failure account, evidence; optionally against a request already open on the pump. Same document and workflow as a dealer's claim. Its own ticket at Pending Review; the dealer is told it was opened for them.
VisitAgainst a request open on the pump, or say what it is for and one is opened typed as a visit. Technician, date, type, a note. The request's ticket, with the booking on the thread.

The desk's own Create button on the ticket list comes here too: a ticket with no pump on it is not a KUMAR ticket. A claim needs a dealer on record; a pump sold direct with no registration is told so and the sale is registered first.

KUMAR team

Tickets

Every request and every claim is a ticket, and the ticket is the one screen.

What kind of ticket it is is the first thing on it. Under the title sit a coloured chip for the kind — Complaint Warranty Claim, Installation, Spare Part, Enquiry — the warranty verdict, the serial, and how it reached KUMAR (via Dealer Portal, via Phone, WhatsApp, Email, Walk-in). The ticket list carries the same chip in its Type column, so a queue reads as complaints, claims and visits rather than a wall of subjects. A ticket a member of staff raised for a caller opens with a line saying so: Raised by KUMAR (Ravi) for Deccan Pumps, via Phone — the dealer sees at once that KUMAR took the call.

The right-hand panel, KUMAR Pumps, sits above the ticket's own fields and shows the job behind the ticket without leaving the conversation:

  • The pump — serial, model, dealer, where it is installed, and the warranty verdict. The serial opens Pump Lookup.
  • The request — type, category, priority, who it is for, the technician, and the two SLA clocks in words: Reply due Thursday turns into Replied Thursday the moment an agent answers.
  • The claim, if there is one — stage, amounts, and the workflow buttons your role is allowed. A request's ticket points at the claim's ticket and back.
  • Visits — every one booked or done, and a Schedule button that books a technician and tells the dealer on this thread.
One thread, two doors

The conversation on the ticket and the conversation on the request are the same conversation. A dealer's message through the portal, a visit booking, a claim decision — each appears on the ticket. An agent's reply typed on the ticket page appears on the request, stamps the SLA first response, and if a dealer replies on a ticket that was already resolved, the request reopens. Each message crosses exactly once.

Reply in the desk, not the comment box

A reply typed into a document's plain comment box skips the notification and the SLA first-response stamp — the dealer never hears about it. The comment box is hidden on these documents for exactly that reason.

Claims desk

/kumar-desk/claims — decide a claim without leaving the desk.

Money by stage across the top; under it every claim with the evidence the decision rests on — the technician's report, the root cause, and the heat and winding batch, because "is this a manufacturing defect" is answered by the batch as often as the report. Each card says who raised it and who it is for, with the customer's number.

Open conversation takes you to the claim's ticket: the full ticket screen, with the dealer's photographs and video, your replies and attachments, and the KUMAR panel beside it — not a chat box in a card. Schedule a visit books a technician straight from the claim; a claim that has no service request yet gets one opened and linked, so the visit has somewhere to be recorded.

FromActionWho may
Pending ReviewSend for investigation / RejectService Manager
Under InvestigationApprove (with an amount) / RejectQuality Engineer

Buttons appear only for actions your role can complete, and the server refuses the rest. An approval cannot exceed the amount claimed. Every decision tells the dealer; on a rejection they are owed the reason, so the box asks for it.

Visits

/kumar-desk/visits — who is waiting, who is booked, who can go.

KUMAR schedules on behalf of whoever owns the pump, including customers who bought direct and have no login. Every waiting row shows the customer, the site address, how long it has been open, and whether it is under warranty.

1Press Schedule on the row The warranty position is shown again in the dialog, so chargeability is decided before a technician is sent rather than argued about afterwards.
2Pick the technician and the date Chargeable defaults from the warranty, not from whoever is filling the form.

Pump Lookup

/kumar-desk/lookup — any serial, for whoever is on the phone.

Leads with the verdict: In warranty Expiring soon Out of warranty or Not registered, with the days left and whether the visit is chargeable said in words, and a bar showing how much cover is spent. Under it: whose pump, which heat and winding batch it came from, and everything that has already gone wrong with it. A repeat failure is called one.

Raise for this pump hands the serial to Raise for a Pump. Individual pump owners never need a login; the team carries their call for them. A ticket's KUMAR panel links here with the serial already looked up.

Admin

Running it

One command brings the whole thing up, or back up.

/home/frappe/frappe-bench/start-kumar.sh          # start whatever is down
/home/frappe/frappe-bench/start-kumar.sh status   # just report

It starts Redis, the web server on :8003, the worker and the scheduler, only where each is not already running, and switches the scheduler on — a restored site comes back with it off, and the scheduler is what sends warranty reminders and flags SLA breaches.

Run this before any demo

The bench's Redis instances have died on a SIGTERM before. When they do, every page returns 500 and nothing in the app looks wrong except that none of it works.

The demo data

Make it tell the story of a working desk, relative to today.

bench --site kumarpumps.localhost execute \
  kumar_service.setup.demo_story.build_all

The seed left forty-six requests of which none had ever been resolved and forty-three had blown their SLA — the data of a company whose service desk does not work. This reshapes it into a healthy month: most requests resolved inside SLA, a handful open and answered on time, two or three genuinely late so the alerting is seen to work, visits booked for the coming week, some warranties about to lapse, claims at every stage.

It is seeded and idempotent — run it the morning of a demo, as often as you like, and the same picture comes back dated to today. It marks what it creates so it can take it back, and touches nothing on a site the demo seed did not build.

Who sees what

Scoping is enforced in two places, and both are needed.

A permission query condition scopes a list. A has_permission hook scopes reading one document by name. A doctype with only the first leaks the second way.

DoctypeA dealer sees
DealerTheir own outlet and its sub-dealers.
Serial NoSerials their tree sold. Read only — a pump is created in the plant.
Service Request / Warranty ClaimTheir tree's own.
Service VisitVisits on their own tickets.
Pump Test CertificateCertificates for pumps their tree sold.
HD TicketTickets they raised or are the contact on, plus their outlet's.
Widening a doctype means checking every path that reads it

Three separate leaks in this project came from granting a role a doctype without checking the endpoints that read it with frappe.db, which applies no row scoping. frappe.get_all ignores permissions too; use frappe.get_list wherever a user's own scope should apply.

Reports

Twelve, all file-based and version-controlled.

  • Stock vs Registration Reconciliation — pumps shipped but never registered.
  • Dealer Requests and Claims — everything raised, by dealer and channel.
  • Model Reliability — failures per thousand sold, by model.
  • Batch Defect Analysis — failures traced back to a heat or winding batch.
  • Warranty Cost Analysis, Dealer Performance, Serial Genealogy, SLA Compliance, Technician Productivity, Warranty Expiring Soon, Unregistered Stock, Heat Chemistry Log.

Each is addressed to specific roles; a Service Manager sees the service reports and not the foundry's. They need no server_script_enabled switch.

Admin

Traps worth knowing

Things that will cost an afternoon if nobody wrote them down.

Email is wired, not configured

Requests and claims notify through the same path and will email the moment an outgoing Email Account exists. Until then the Notification Log fills with "setup default outgoing" errors and nothing is sent.

HSN codes are mandatory

India Compliance requires a 6- or 8-digit gst_hsn_code on every Item. Pumps 841370, parts 84139190, motors 850152 — have the accountant confirm before the first return. 143 pre-existing items still have none.

Restart after installing an app

The dev server holds stale hooks. A 500 on every page straight after an install is almost always this.

The site needs its own port

serve_default_site is true bench-wide, so the port decides the site, not the Host header. The start script handles it.

Camera scanning is not universal

It uses BarcodeDetector — Chrome and Edge on Android have it, Firefox and iOS Safari do not. The text box and a USB scanner always work.

Expiring soon is 30 days

Defined once, in kumar_service.utils.EXPIRING_SOON_DAYS. Every screen reads it; two of them used to say 45.

A claim's ticket does not carry the request's field

A claim ticket links to its request only through the claim. If it also carried custom_service_request, the request would have two tickets, and every "find the ticket for this request" — the mirror, status sync, the reply bridge — could land on the wrong one. The mirror leaves it empty on purpose, and the lookups exclude claim tickets as insurance.

How the bridge tells its own messages apart

Messages the bridge writes onto a ticket carry medium Chat and a message id of kumar-comment:<comment>; the reverse hook skips those, so nothing echoes. Moving a claim's ticket status does not move the claim — claims move only through their workflow buttons.
